# THE LEAF-MOTE
*the Reading Hall of Open Leaves*


Sunlight spills through the woven canopy of the great oak that shelters the hall, turning the air into a shifting lattice of gold and green. The scent of old parchment and crushed moss mingles with the faint, sweet perfume of night-blooming jasmine, and a quiet rustle—like a hundred pages turning at once—fills the silence between breaths.

## What happens here

At dawn and dusk, the scholars of Aelindor gather here for the *spoken reading*, where one of their number recites from the living archive of the grove—the leaves of the oak, which unfurl new verses each day under the whispering of a gentle breeze. The hall is open to any who wish to listen, and the readers are chosen by drawing of lots from among the town's citizens, ensuring no voice holds sway too long.

## Where it sits

The Leaf-Mote is an open-air hall built around the massive trunk of an ancient oak in the heart of the Whispering-Deep, a grove of tiered gardens and shallow pools that wraps around the base of a gentle hill. A narrow path of white stones leads from the eastern gate of the hill town of Aelindor, winding through the grove to the hall's single arched entrance.

## Features

A single circular chamber roughly thirty paces across, ringed by slender pillars of pale wood that rise to a dome of interwoven branches and leaves. At the center, the oak's trunk serves as a lectern, its bark carved with spiraling script that gleams faintly in the dim light. Low benches of polished stone curve around the trunk, each one worn smooth by countless hands, and small copper lamps hang from the branches, unlit but ready for dusk.

## Who frequents here

Elarawn, a retired archivist with a voice like cracked honey, comes every dawn to sweep the fallen leaves and to read aloud the newest lines, his fingers tracing the bark-script as if bidding farewell to an old friend. He was once a reader himself, chosen by the lots nine harvests running, and he still carries the memory of the morning he read a verse that seemed to answer a grief he had never spoken to anyone—least of all himself. His daughter, Liriel, a young scribe with ink-stained fingers, often joins him at dusk, sitting on the eastern bench where she first heard him read, and where she now argues with him gently about the meaning of a particularly stubborn leaf.

## Hidden detail

On the inner curve of the easternmost pillar, just above the reach of a seated person, a single leaf has been carved into the stone and painted a deep, bruised violet—a color that does not fade, though no one admits to having seen the carver, and a small tally of vertical scratches sits beside it, nine marks deep, that no one admits to keeping.

## NPCs

### ELARAWN
:

### LIRIEL
:

A young scribe with ink-stained fingers, Elarawn's daughter, who joins him at dusk on the eastern bench to debate the meaning of the leaves, her loyalty to the hall born of that first shared reading.
::

### THE ORDER OF THE UNLIT LAMP
:

A nameless conclave of archivists and lamplighters who maintain the hall's unlit copper lamps. They act through subtle influence, funding repairs and selecting which visitors are allowed to sit close to the trunk, believing the oak's order must be perpetually curated.
::

### GRAI THE EMBER
:

Grai the Ember, a one-armed charcoal-burner who lost his fingers to a fire he says the oak itself conjured, leads a rage-fueled splinter of outcasts. He preaches that the grove is a parasite on the hill-town's true people and that the hall should be left to rot into a memorial, not a gathering place.
::

### VEILEN
:

Veilen, a scarred gardener who once tended the grove's night-blooming flowers, leads a band of disenfranchised moss-gatherers. He preaches that the hall's quiet beauty is a gilded cage, and that the oak's power should be commanded, not cherished.

## Quests
::

### The Violet Leaf's Unraveling
:

{{descriptive
 As the last light of dusk filters through the oak's canopy, you find Liriel seated alone on the eastern bench, a half-unrolled scroll on her lap. She looks up, ink-smudged and earnest, and beckons you closer with a quiet urgency. 'You've come at the right hour,' she says, 'the leaves are restless tonight, and I need someone who can listen.'
:

Liriel explains that the oak's daily verses have been growing fragmented and strange—words missing, letters reversed, and occasional bursts of nonsense that unsettle the regulars. She's convinced it's not natural. She asks you to investigate the grove and the hall's history, to uncover what's causing the disturbance and restore the oak's voice. 'The leaves are writing a warning,' she says, 'or a plea. I need you to find out which—and why.' 
}}
:

#### Quest Giver: Liriel
:

A young scribe with ink-stained fingers, Elarawn's daughter, who joins him at dusk on the eastern bench to debate the meaning of the leaves, her loyalty to the hall born of that first shared reading.
:

#### Objectives
:

- Search the archives of the Leaf-Mote and Aelindor for records of previous disturbances to the oak's verses.
- Examine the carved violet leaf on the eastern pillar and its nine tally marks, looking for clues about its origin and meaning.
- Interview long-time readers and caretakers, including Elarawn, about any changes they've noticed in the grove or the hall.
- Decipher the fragmented verses by cross-referencing them with older leaves stored in the hall's collection, searching for a hidden pattern.
#### Challenges
:

- The older records are written in a faded, archaic script and are scattered across several private collections in Aelindor, requiring persuasion or favors to access.
- A rival scholar from a nearby town has begun spreading rumors that the oak's verses are simply deteriorating with age, undermining Liriel's concerns and drawing away potential helpers.
- The fragmented verses, when pieced together, seem to describe a ritual involving the violet leaf—but the final piece is protected by a warding glyph that resists simple dispelling.
#### Rewards
:

- Liriel offers a rare folio of pressed leaves from the oak's first century, said to be a prized possession of her father, as a token of gratitude.
- The oak itself seems to respond to your efforts—you gain a small boon, such as a brief vision of the grove's past or a luck charm made from a fallen leaf that glows faintly.
- Elarawn, grateful for your help, uses his influence to secure a letter of introduction from the town's archivist, which grants access to restricted archives elsewhere in the region.
#### Twist
:

The disturbance is not natural—it is the work of a remorseful former reader who, years ago, attempted to carve a new verse into the oak and was rejected by the tree. The carved violet leaf and the nine tally marks are his silent confession, a vigil he keeps every dawn. He believes the oak is dying and has been trying to warn the town through the fragmented verses, but his guilt has twisted the message into incoherence. The players must decide whether to expose him, help him, or find another path to heal the oak.
:

### The Violet Leaf's Debt
:

{{descriptive
 You find Grai the Ember in a smoke-stained clearing at the edge of the charcoal-burners' quarter, crouched over a low fire that he feeds with twisted twigs. He looks up, his one hand resting on a charred hatchet handle. 'So you're the ones who listen,' he rasps. 'Good. I need people who can hear a lie and still cut it down.'
:

Grai gestures toward the distant canopy of the Whispering-Deep. 'That oak is a liar. It sings verses to keep us tame while it drinks the hill's blood. But I've found its weakness—a violet leaf carved on the eastern pillar, nine marks beside it. Those marks are years the oak has cheated death. It keeps a guardian, a thing made of rot and stolen breath, that hunts anyone who reads too true. I want it dead. Kill the guardian, break the marks, and prove the grove can be ended. If you do this, the town will see it's just a tree—not a god.' He spits into the fire. 'And I'll make it worth your while.' 
}}
:

#### Quest Giver: Grai the Ember
:

Grai the Ember is a one-armed charcoal-burner who lost his fingers and his livelihood to a fire he insists was conjured by the great oak itself. Once a respected artisan, he now leads a small, bitter faction of outcasts who believe the grove is a parasite feeding on the hill-town's true people. He is persuasive, sharp-tongued, and his hatred for the oak is both personal and ideological.
:

#### Objectives
:

- Track the guardian to its lair, a collapsed root-cellar beneath the eastern gate of Aelindor.
- Infiltrate the Leaf-Mote at dusk and disrupt the spoken reading to draw the guardian out.
- Slay the guardian, a rot-veined stalker that can mimic the voices of the scholars.
- Scrape away the nine tally marks beside the violet leaf on the eastern pillar.
#### Challenges
:

- The guardian is nearly invisible in the shifting light of the grove and only shows itself when it mimics a familiar voice.
- The town's sentinels patrol the grove at dusk; a failed stealth check raises the alarm and halts the ritual.
- Grai's followers are jumpy and will attack the party if they suspect the party is helping the oak instead of hunting it.
#### Rewards
:

- A purse of 200 silver marks and Grai's promise of safe passage through the charcoal-burners' quarter.
- A hand-charred charm that grants advantage on saving throws against being frightened by fire, carved by Grai himself.
- The gratitude of the Leaf-Mote's keepers, who grant the party free access to the living archive's oldest scrolls, one of which hints at the oak's true bargain.
#### Twist
:

When the party breaks the ninth mark, the violet leaf turns to ash, and the great oak groans—its verses twist into a curse. A vision reveals that the tally marks were not years of the oak's life, but a warding against a far older horror sealed beneath the hill; the marks were placed by the oak's allies. The oak is not the parasite—it is the binding. Grai, unknowing, has unleashed the thing the marks were keeping at bay. Now the party must decide: repair the warding or face the consequence of their hunt.
:
